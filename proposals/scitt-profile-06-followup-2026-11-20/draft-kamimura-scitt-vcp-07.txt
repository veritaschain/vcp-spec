A SCITT Profile for Verifiable Audit Trails in Algorithmic Trading: The
                      VeritasChain Protocol (VCP)
                      draft-kamimura-scitt-vcp-07

Abstract

   This document defines a profile of the SCITT (Supply Chain Integrity,
   Transparency, and Trust) architecture for creating tamper-evident
   audit trails of AI-driven algorithmic trading decisions and
   executions.  The VeritasChain Protocol (VCP) applies the SCITT
   framework to address the specific requirements of financial markets,
   including high-precision timestamps, considerations arising from
   recordkeeping regimes such as the EU AI Act and MiFID II, and a
   crypto-shredding mechanism intended to support erasure obligations
   such as those under the GDPR.  This profile specifies how VCP events
   are encoded as SCITT Signed Statements, registered with Transparency
   Services, and verified using COSE Receipts.  It further defines SCITT
   conformance profiles for interoperability and an ERASURE event type
   that records crypto-shredding operations as append-only audit events.
   The profile makes trading records auditable and attributable after
   the fact; it does not prevent or block any trading activity, and it
   does not warrant the correctness of any recorded decision.

1.  Introduction

   The SCITT (Supply Chain Integrity, Transparency, and Trust)
   architecture [RFC9943] provides a framework for creating tamper-
   evident logs of digital artifacts through Transparency Services.
   While SCITT was initially designed for software supply chain use
   cases, its core primitives--Signed Statements, Receipts, and
   Transparency Services--are applicable to any domain requiring
   verifiable audit trails.




Kamimura                   Expires 28 May 2027                  [Page 3]

Internet-Draft                  SCITT-VCP                  November 2026


   This document specifies a SCITT profile for AI-driven algorithmic
   trading systems in financial markets.  The VeritasChain Protocol
   (VCP) applies the SCITT architecture with domain-specific extensions:

   *  A schema for encoding trading events as SCITT Signed Statements

   *  Registration policies for financial audit trails

   *  Conformance tiers (Silver, Gold, Platinum) with varying
      requirements for timing precision, anchoring frequency, and key
      protection

   *  A crypto-shredding mechanism intended to support data erasure
      obligations

   *  A completeness-aware design that enables omission detection
      through external anchoring

   VCP serves as an "AI Flight Recorder" for algorithmic trading,
   supporting post-incident reconstruction of recorded system behavior
   with cryptographic evidence that the records were not altered after
   commitment.  It cannot reconstruct events that were never recorded.

1.2.  Relationship to SCITT

   This document is a profile of the SCITT architecture.  It:

   *  REQUIRES compliance with [RFC9943]

   *  REQUIRES use of SCRAPI [I-D.ietf-scitt-scrapi] for Transparency
      Service interactions

   *  RECOMMENDS COSE Receipts for inclusion proofs

   *  DEFINES domain-specific extensions for financial trading

   This profile does not define a new SCITT protocol or new Transparency
   Service interactions; it specifies how existing SCITT primitives are
   applied to VCP events in the financial trading domain.  The VCP event
   format and integrity layer are defined in [VCP-SPEC].

1.3.  Scope

   This document specifies:

   *  VCP Event schema as SCITT Signed Statement payload

   *  Registration Policy requirements for VCP Transparency Services

   *  Mapping of VCP concepts to SCITT terminology

   *  Three conformance tiers with specific requirements

   *  Crypto-shredding mechanism to support erasure obligations

   *  Three-layer integrity architecture

   This document does not specify:

   *  General SCITT architecture (see [RFC9943])

   *  SCRAPI endpoints (see [I-D.ietf-scitt-scrapi])

   *  COSE Receipt format (see [RFC9942])

   *  Specific regulatory mapping (covered in companion documents)

   *  A particular anchoring service (the anchor target is deployment-
      specific; anchoring itself is REQUIRED)

2.  Terminology

   This document uses terminology from [RFC9943].  The following terms
   are specific to this profile:

   VCP Event  A single auditable record in the VeritasChain Protocol,
      encoded as a SCITT Signed Statement.  Consists of Header, Payload,
      and Security metadata.

   VCP Issuer  An algorithmic trading system, AI model, or human
      operator that generates VCP Events.  Corresponds to SCITT Issuer.

   VCP Transparency Service  A SCITT Transparency Service configured
      with VCP-specific Registration Policies.  Operates a VCP-compliant
      append-only log.

   VCP Receipt  A COSE Receipt issued by a VCP Transparency Service,
      proving inclusion of a VCP Event in the log.




Kamimura                   Expires 28 May 2027                  [Page 5]

Internet-Draft                  SCITT-VCP                  November 2026


   Actor  An entity (algorithm, human operator, or system) identified by
      ActorID that generates VCP Events.

   Crypto-Shredding  A privacy technique where encrypted payload data is
      rendered permanently unrecoverable by destroying the encryption
      key, while preserving the cryptographic integrity proofs (hashes
      and Receipts).

   Conformance Tier  One of three implementation levels (Silver, Gold,
      Platinum) with increasing requirements for timing precision,
      anchoring frequency, and key protection.

   External Anchor  A cryptographic commitment (typically a Merkle root)
      published to an external, independent system (e.g., public
      blockchain, timestamping authority).  External anchoring makes
      omission of events from an anchored batch, after anchoring,
      detectable by third parties at batch granularity.

   Policy Identification  A field within the VCP Event that explicitly
      identifies the applicable Registration Policy and conformance
      tier.

3.1.  Event Flow

   1.  *Event Generation:* VCP Issuer creates a VCP Event containing
       trading decision or execution data.

   2.  *Signing:* Event is signed using COSE_Sign1 [RFC9052] with the
       Issuer's private key.

   3.  *Registration:* Signed Statement is submitted to VCP Transparency
       Service via SCRAPI POST /entries.

   4.  *Validation:* Transparency Service validates against VCP
       Registration Policy.

   5.  *Logging:* Valid statement is appended to the append-only log.

   6.  *Receipt:* COSE Receipt with Merkle inclusion proof is returned
       to Issuer.

   7.  *External Anchoring:* The Merkle root MUST be periodically
       anchored to an external system at the frequency of the applicable
       tier (Section 5.3), making post-anchor omission detectable by
       third parties.

3.2.  Per-Actor Hash Chain (Optional)

   VCP MAY maintain per-Actor hash chains in addition to SCITT's global
   append-only log.  When implemented, each VCP Event includes a
   PrevHash field containing the hash of the previous event from the
   same Actor.  This enables efficient verification of a single Actor's
   event sequence without downloading the entire log.

   Note: Per-Actor hash chains are OPTIONAL.  External anchoring of
   batch roots provides the primary mechanism for completeness
   verification at batch granularity.  Hash chains serve as a
   complementary local integrity mechanism.

     Actor A:  Event_A1 --hash--> Event_A2 --hash--> Event_A3

     Actor B:  Event_B1 --hash--> Event_B2

     Global Log: [Event_A1, Event_B1, Event_A2, Event_B2, Event_A3, ...]

3.3.  Three-Layer Integrity Architecture

   VCP defines a three-layer architecture that separates concerns and
   clarifies where each integrity property originates:

        +=======+===============+===================+=============+
        | Layer | Component     | Property          | Protocol    |
        |       |               |                   | Requirement |
        +=======+===============+===================+=============+
        | Layer | Event         | Individual event  | REQUIRED    |
        | 1     | Integrity     | authenticity via  |             |
        |       |               | digital signature |             |
        +-------+---------------+-------------------+-------------+
        | Layer | Collection    | Merkle tree over  | REQUIRED    |
        | 2     | Integrity     | event collection  |             |
        +-------+---------------+-------------------+-------------+
        | Layer | External      | Merkle root       | REQUIRED    |
        | 3     | Verifiability | anchored to       |             |
        |       |               | external system   |             |
        +-------+---------------+-------------------+-------------+

                   Table 2: VCP Three-Layer Architecture

   Note: Layer 3 (External Verifiability) is REQUIRED at every tier, as
   in VCP v1.2 [VCP-SPEC].  Revisions -02 and -03 of this document
   described it as OPTIONAL at the protocol level; that description did
   not match the VCP specification and is withdrawn.  Lightweight or
   delegated anchoring mechanisms are acceptable at the Silver tier.




Kamimura                   Expires 28 May 2027                  [Page 9]

Internet-Draft                  SCITT-VCP                  November 2026


   External anchoring makes post-anchor omission detectable: without it,
   a log producer could rebuild a batch and publish a different Merkle
   root.  Anchoring lets third parties check a presented root against an
   independently recorded commitment, supporting the "Verify, Don't
   Trust" principle.  It does not reveal events that were never included
   in a batch.

     Layer 1: Event Integrity
     +--------+    +--------+    +--------+
     | Event1 |    | Event2 |    | Event3 |
     | (sig)  |    | (sig)  |    | (sig)  |
     +---+----+    +---+----+    +---+----+
         |            |            |
         v            v            v
     Layer 2: Collection Integrity (Merkle Tree)
     +------------------------------------------+
     |              Merkle Root                 |
     +---------------------+--------------------+
                           |
                           v (REQUIRED)
     Layer 3: External Verifiability
     +------------------------------------------+
     | External Anchor (Blockchain/TSA/etc.)    |
     +------------------------------------------+

4.  VCP Event Schema

   A VCP Event is encoded as the payload of a SCITT Signed Statement.
   The payload MUST be a JSON object conforming to the following schema:

4.1.  Header Object

   The Header contains metadata common to all VCP Events:

   {
     "Header": {
       "EventID": "019cf0d4-be3b-7000-8000-123456789abc",
       "TimestampISO": "2026-03-15T09:30:00.123456789Z",
       "TimestampInt": 1773567000123456789,
       "EventType": "ORD",
       "ActorID": "algo-momentum-001",
       "ChainID": "chain-actor-001",
       "SequenceNum": 42,
       "PolicyID": "org.veritaschain.policy:silver-v1-2"
     }
   }

   EventID  REQUIRED.  UUID v7 [RFC9562] providing time-sortable unique



Kamimura                   Expires 28 May 2027                 [Page 10]

Internet-Draft                  SCITT-VCP                  November 2026


      identification.

   TimestampISO  REQUIRED.  ISO 8601 timestamp with nanosecond
      precision.

   TimestampInt  REQUIRED.  Integer timestamp in nanoseconds since Unix
      epoch.  Values exceed 2^53, so a parser that does not preserve
      64-bit integer precision alters them.  See Section 9 regarding
      canonicalization.

   EventType  REQUIRED.  Registered event type code (see Section 4.5).

   ActorID  REQUIRED.  Identifier of the entity generating this event.

   ChainID  REQUIRED.  Identifier of the per-Actor hash chain.

   SequenceNum  REQUIRED.  Monotonically increasing sequence number
      within the Actor's chain.

   PolicyID  REQUIRED.  Identifier of the Registration Policy under
      which the event is registered.  Per VCP v1.2 [VCP-SPEC], PolicyID
      SHOULD follow the Issuer Domain + Local ID convention
      (reverse_domain:local_identifier), for example
      "org.veritaschain.policy:gold-v1-2".  Global uniqueness is
      achieved through domain ownership; no central PolicyID registry is
      operated.

4.2.  Payload Object

   The Payload contains domain-specific data organized into modules:

   VCP-TRADE  Trading data: orders, executions, modifications,
      cancellations.

   VCP-RISK  Risk management data: exposure, limits, margin.

   VCP-GOV  Governance data: algorithm parameters, decision factors,
      operator actions.

   VCP-PRIVACY  Privacy metadata: encryption keys, retention policies.

     "Payload": {

         "OrderID": "ord-2026-001",

4.3.  Security Object

   The Security object contains integrity and chaining information:

   {
     "Security": {
       "EventHash": "sha256:a1b2c3d4...",
       "PrevHash": "sha256:f6e5d4c3...",
       "MerkleRoot": "sha256:1234abcd...",
       "SignAlgo": "ED25519",
       "AnchorRef": "eth:0x1234...@12345678"
     }
   }

   EventHash  REQUIRED.  SHA-256 hash of the canonicalized Header and
      Payload, computed using JSON Canonicalization Scheme [RFC8785].

   PrevHash  OPTIONAL.  Hash of the previous event in this Actor's
      chain.  If present, MUST match the EventHash of the preceding
      event (except for INIT events where it is absent).

   MerkleRoot  OPTIONAL in individual events.  Included in anchor (ANC)
      events.

   SignAlgo  REQUIRED.  Signature algorithm identifier.  MUST be
      "ED25519" (default) or "DILITHIUM2", the VCP v1.2 registry value
      for ML-DSA [FIPS-204].  DILITHIUM2 carries EXPERIMENTAL status in
      VCP v1.2 and is intended for testing and hybrid (classical plus
      post-quantum) deployments; it is not a certification requirement.




Kamimura                   Expires 28 May 2027                 [Page 12]

Internet-Draft                  SCITT-VCP                  November 2026


   AnchorRef  OPTIONAL.  Reference to the anchor location (e.g.,
      blockchain transaction ID, TSA receipt ID), populated once the
      batch containing the event has been anchored.  See Section 9 on
      the corresponding field in [VCP-SPEC].

4.4.  SCITT Alignment Object

   VCP v1.2 [VCP-SPEC] defines an OPTIONAL SCITTAlignment object carried
   in the Header.  It makes the binding between a VCP Event and its
   SCITT artifacts (Signed Statement, Transparency Service, Receipt)
   explicit and machine-readable.

   Field presence is governed by conformance profiles:

      +================+================+==========================+
      | Profile        | SCITTAlignment | Intended Use             |
      +================+================+==========================+
      | VCP_BASE       | OPTIONAL       | Standard VCP deployment  |
      +----------------+----------------+--------------------------+
      | VCP_SCITT      | REQUIRED       | SCITT Transparency       |
      |                |                | Service interoperability |
      +----------------+----------------+--------------------------+
      | VCP_REGULATORY | RECOMMENDED    | Regulatory reporting     |
      |                |                | scenarios                |
      +----------------+----------------+--------------------------+

              Table 3: SCITT Alignment Conformance Profiles

   Implementations claiming the VCP_SCITT profile MUST include the
   SCITTAlignment object in every registered event; this profile-based
   approach avoids partial-implementation ambiguity.

   {
     "Header": {
       "SCITTAlignment": {
         "Version": "1.2",
         "ConformanceProfile": "VCP_SCITT",
         "StatementID": "stmt-01961e5f-5c0d-7000",
         "TransparencyServiceID": "ts.veritaschain.example",
         "ReceiptFormat": "COSE_RECEIPT",
         "RegistrationPolicy": {
           "PolicyID": "org.veritaschain.policy:gold-v1-2",
           "Issuer": "org.veritaschain",
           "IssuedAt": 1767225600000000000
         }
       }
     }
   }

   Version  REQUIRED when present.  VCP specification version governing

   ConformanceProfile  REQUIRED when present.  One of "VCP_BASE",

5.  Registration Policy

   A VCP Transparency Service MUST enforce a Registration Policy that
   validates incoming Signed Statements.

5.1.  Policy Identification

   The PolicyID field MUST be explicitly included in the VCP Event
   Header.  The Transparency Service MUST validate the registration
   request against the referenced Registration Policy and MUST NOT
   infer, select, or substitute a policy on its own.

   PolicyID values SHOULD follow the Issuer Domain + Local ID convention
   defined in VCP v1.2 [VCP-SPEC]: the reverse DNS notation of the
   Issuer's domain, a colon, and an issuer-defined local identifier.
   Examples:

   *  org.veritaschain.policy:silver-v1-2

   *  org.veritaschain.policy:gold-v1-2

   *  org.veritaschain.policy:platinum-v1-2

   Uniqueness derives from domain ownership; no central PolicyID
   registry is operated.  Organizations without a domain MAY use a
   "local:" prefix at the cost of weaker uniqueness.

   Conformance tiers (Silver, Gold, Platinum) represent additional
   verification depth and operational requirements applied on top of the
   same core Registration Policy, and do not define separate or
   incompatible registration policies.

5.2.  Validation Requirements

   The Registration Policy MUST verify:

   1.  *Schema Compliance:* Payload conforms to VCP Event schema.

   2.  *PolicyID Present:* Header contains a valid PolicyID field.

   3.  *Signature Validity:* COSE_Sign1 signature is valid for the
       registered Issuer public key.

   4.  *Timestamp Validity:* TimestampInt is within acceptable bounds
       (not in future, not too old).

   5.  *Chain Integrity (if PrevHash present):* PrevHash matches the
       hash of the most recent event from this Actor.



Kamimura                   Expires 28 May 2027                 [Page 16]

Internet-Draft                  SCITT-VCP                  November 2026


   6.  *Sequence Monotonicity:* SequenceNum is exactly one greater than
       the previous event's SequenceNum.

5.3.  Tier-Specific Requirements

   accuracy.  Nanosecond-resolution timestamps represent the storage

5.4.  Certification Policy Requirements

   This document does not mandate a specific external anchoring service;
   it requires anchoring at the frequency of each tier.  Each
   AnchorRecord binds the batch Merkle root, event count, first and last
   event identifiers, and PolicyID (see Section 9 on the scope of the
   AnchorRecord signature).  Implementations retain local copies of all
   AnchorRecords and document an anchor continuity plan, as defined in
   [VCP-SPEC].

   Certification programs may impose requirements beyond this profile,
   such as multiple independent anchors.  Certification requirements are
   defined separately from this document and may evolve independently of
   it.  Implementations may conform to this profile without obtaining
   certification.

   Acceptable anchor targets include:

   *  Trusted timestamping authorities (TSA) per [RFC3161]

   *  SCITT Transparency Services, with COSE Receipts per [RFC9942]

   *  Public blockchains (e.g., Bitcoin, Ethereum)

   *  Public timestamping services and attested databases, at the tiers
      where [VCP-SPEC] permits them

   A time-stamping authority attests existence time; by itself it does
   not show that the same root was presented to every verifier.

6.  Crypto-Shredding

   VCP supports crypto-shredding as a technical mechanism that can
   support erasure obligations while preserving audit trail integrity.
   It renders protected plaintext irrecoverable without invalidating
   cryptographic proofs.  Whether it satisfies a specific legal
   obligation is not determined by this document.

6.1.  Mechanism

   1.  *Encryption:* Sensitive payload fields are encrypted with a per-
       record or per-subject Data Encryption Key (DEK).

   2.  *Key Storage:* DEK is stored separately, indexed by SubjectID.

   3.  *Hashing:* EventHash is computed over the encrypted payload,
       preserving integrity.




Kamimura                   Expires 28 May 2027                 [Page 18]

Internet-Draft                  SCITT-VCP                  November 2026


   4.  *Shredding:* Upon erasure request, DEK is destroyed.  Encrypted
       data becomes unrecoverable.

   5.  *Verification:* Receipt and hash chain remain valid--verifiers
       can confirm event existence and ordering without accessing
       decrypted content.

6.2.  VCP-PRIVACY Module

   {
     "VCP-PRIVACY": {
       "EncryptedFields": ["VCP-TRADE.ClientID", "VCP-TRADE.AccountID"],
       "KeyID": "dek-2026-001-subject-12345",
       "Algorithm": "AES-256-GCM",
       "RetentionPolicy": "GDPR-5Y"
     }
   }

6.3.  ERASURE Event Type

   VCP v1.2 [VCP-SPEC] defines the ERASURE event type to reconcile
   erasure obligations (such as GDPR Article 17) with append-only,
   tamper-evident audit trails.  An ERASURE event does not delete or
   rewrite any prior event.  It records, as a new append-only event,
   that the plaintext of specified protected fields has been rendered
   irrecoverable through crypto-shredding (destruction of the Data
   Encryption Key), while the EventHash, Merkle inclusion, and external
   anchor of the target events remain verifiable.

   In the VCP v1.2 release, an ERASURE event MUST carry its details in
   Payload.VCP-PRIVACY.ErasureDetails, with the seven REQUIRED members
   below.  ErasureDetails at the event root or directly under Payload is
   invalid.  The flat ERASURE fields described in -03 of this document
   (ErasureTargetEventIDs, ErasureReason, KeyDestructionProof,
   RetentionExemption, OperatorID) reflected pre-release artifacts and
   are not a v1.2 encoding.  Historical signed data is not rewritten.
   The normative annex of [VCP-SPEC] states how pre-release records are
   handled; it defines no lossless automatic conversion to the v1.2
   encoding.

7.  SCRAPI Integration

   VCP Transparency Services MUST implement SCRAPI
   [I-D.ietf-scitt-scrapi].  The descriptions below follow revision -11
   of that API; they do not define alternative SCRAPI resources.

7.1.  Registration (POST /entries)

   A client submits a COSE_Sign1 Signed Statement carrying the VCP event
   to the registration resource defined in Section 2.3 of
   [I-D.ietf-scitt-scrapi].  The Transparency Service applies its
   Registration Policy before further processing.

   POST /entries HTTP/1.1
   Host: vcp-ts.example.com
   Accept: application/cbor
   Accept: application/cose
   Content-Type: application/cose

   <COSE_Sign1 containing VCP Event payload>

   A successful synchronous registration returns the Receipt in a 201
   response.  An accepted asynchronous registration returns 202 while
   the Receipt is pending.  In both cases the Location header identifies
   the Receipt resource.  A 202 response is not evidence that
   registration is complete or that a Receipt has been issued.



Kamimura                   Expires 28 May 2027                 [Page 22]

Internet-Draft                  SCITT-VCP                  November 2026


7.2.  Receipt Resolution (GET the Location URL)

   The client follows the Location URL returned by registration, for
   example /entries/{EntryID}, as defined in Section 2.4 of
   [I-D.ietf-scitt-scrapi].  This resource returns a Receipt, not the
   original VCP event.  A 200 response carries the COSE Receipt; 204
   indicates that registration is still pending; 404 indicates that no
   Receipt exists, including a failed registration.  Polling and error
   handling follow SCRAPI.

   This profile defines no derivation of EntryID from Header.EventID.
   The client uses the service-provided Location instead of synthesizing
   a Receipt URL from an event identifier.

7.3.  Receipt Verification and Statement Availability

   The Receipt resource is the Location URL described above; this
   profile does not append a /receipt suffix.  Obtaining a Receipt is
   separate from verifying it against the candidate Signed Statement and
   the trusted service context.  The unresolved context questions are
   recorded in Section 9, item 6.

   The original Signed Statement and any detached payload are supplied
   to a verifier through the applicable evidence-distribution or
   artifact-repository mechanism.  The SCRAPI Receipt resource does not
   provide an event-download API.

7.4.  External Anchor Information

   External anchor evidence, such as a blockchain reference or timestamp
   token, is made available through the deployment's documented
   evidence-distribution mechanism.  This profile does not define GET
   /anchors/{anchor_id} as a SCRAPI resource.  A deployment-specific
   anchor-status API is outside this profile; its response is not by
   itself verification of the anchor.

8.  Security Considerations

8.1.  Integrity Architecture

   VCP's three-layer architecture provides defense in depth:

   *  *Layer 1 (Event Integrity):* Digital signatures on individual
      events make forgery without the signing key detectable.

   *  *Layer 2 (Collection Integrity):* Merkle trees enable efficient
      proof of inclusion.




Kamimura                   Expires 28 May 2027                 [Page 23]

Internet-Draft                  SCITT-VCP                  November 2026


   *  *Layer 3 (External Verifiability):* External anchoring makes post-
      anchor omission detectable by enabling third parties to check
      presented Merkle roots against independent records.

   The optional per-Actor hash chain provides additional local integrity
   checks but is not required for external verifiability.

   Mitigations against key compromise:

   *  Frequent Merkle anchoring to external append-only stores

   *  HSM-based key storage (Platinum tier requirement)

   *  Key lifecycle management under the deployment's documented policy.
      This profile does not define a ROTATE event or a key-rotation
      protocol.

8.2.  Quantum Resistance

   Ed25519 signatures are vulnerable to attacks by cryptographically
   relevant quantum computers.  VCP provides crypto-agility to address
   future threats:

   *  SignAlgo field enables algorithm negotiation and migration

   *  The DILITHIUM2 registry value (ML-DSA [FIPS-204]) holds
      EXPERIMENTAL status in VCP v1.2: available for testing and hybrid
      (classical plus post-quantum) deployments, with Ed25519 remaining
      the default and no certification requirement attached

   *  Hash chain integrity based on SHA-256 provides approximately
      128-bit post-quantum security against Grover's algorithm

   Implementers requiring post-quantum assurance SHOULD monitor CFRG and
   PQUIP working group outputs for updated guidance on algorithm
   selection and migration timelines.

8.3.  Timing Attacks

   Clock manipulation can enable backdating of events.  Mitigations:

   *  Compare the UUID v7 millisecond timestamp with TimestampInt at
      millisecond precision.  Neither producer-supplied value is an
      independent attestation of the event time.

   *  Transparency Service enforces timestamp bounds

   *  Platinum tier requires PTPv2 synchronization



Kamimura                   Expires 28 May 2027                 [Page 24]

Internet-Draft                  SCITT-VCP                  November 2026


   *  External anchoring provides independent timestamp attestation

8.4.  Privacy Considerations

   VCP Events may contain sensitive trading information.  Operators
   SHOULD:

   *  Use crypto-shredding for personal data subject to GDPR

   *  Implement access controls on Transparency Service queries

   *  Consider encrypted payloads for highly sensitive data

8.5.  Completeness Considerations

   SCITT provides inclusion proofs but does not by itself establish
   completeness (i.e., that no events have been omitted).  VCP adds the
   following:

   *  Sequence numbers enable detection of gaps within an Actor's chain

   *  External anchoring of batch roots, with AnchorRecords binding
      event count, first and last event identifiers and PolicyID,
      enables third-party detection of omission after anchoring

   *  Heartbeat events (HBT) provide liveness signals that support
      detection of extended omission periods

   These mechanisms make omission detectable at batch granularity and
   within the anchoring window; they do not establish that every event
   that occurred was recorded.  An event that was never recorded is
   detectable only through another record that implies it, such as an
   order without a corresponding acknowledgment or a counterparty
   record.  The completeness window implied by the tier's anchoring
   frequency should be disclosed when records are presented.
















Kamimura                   Expires 28 May 2027                 [Page 25]

Internet-Draft                  SCITT-VCP                  November 2026


   This profile relies on COSE Receipts [RFC9942] for inclusion only.
   It draws no conclusion about a Signed Statement's position in a
   Transparency Service's log, or about the log's size, from the tree
   size or leaf index that a Receipt carries, and none of the mechanisms
   above depends on them.  For RFC9162_SHA256 a Receipt of inclusion
   establishes neither: the tree size and leaf index are outside the
   signed protected header, and the same inclusion path can recompute
   the signed root under more than one tree size [SCITT-LIST-OI6].  VCP
   takes the order of events from their sequence numbers; Relying
   Parties cannot assume that log order matches issuance order unless
   the Registration Policy advertises it (Section 9.1 of [RFC9943]).
   The AnchorRecord fields listed above are not yet covered by the
   AnchorRecord signature; see item 2 of Section 9.

9.  Open Issues

   A comparison of the VCP v1.2 integrity layer with the VAP v1.2
   framework requirements, published as an unadopted research
   observation record [VCP-VAP-OBS], identified items 1 to 5 below.
   Item 6 was added in revision -06.  This revision settles its part on
   log position and size and leaves its other parts open; items 1 to 5
   are not resolved by this revision.

   1.  Local batch Merkle construction.  [VCP-SPEC] requires Merkle tree
       construction to follow RFC 6962, but its reference code
       duplicates an odd node, which yields roots that differ from those
       of [RFC9162] for batch sizes that are not a power of two, and its
       audit path generation omits that node.  Which construction
       governs, and how roots already computed with the reference code
       are treated, is not decided by this revision.  The question
       concerns local batch trees only: inclusion in a SCITT
       Transparency Service's log is shown by COSE Receipts [RFC9942]
       under the verifiable data structure that the Transparency Service
       uses.

   2.  AnchorRecord signature scope.  The reference signing procedure
       signs the Merkle root bytes only, so the event count, endpoints
       and PolicyID carried in the AnchorRecord are not covered by that
       signature.  The signed bytes of the AnchorRecord remain to be
       defined.  Section 8.5 relies on these fields for omission
       detection after anchoring.  One direction under consideration is
       to register the AnchorRecord itself as a Signed Statement whose
       payload carries them, so that the Issuer's signature covers them;
       it is not adopted.

   3.  Integer timestamps.  TimestampInt and other nanosecond values are
       JSON integers that can exceed 2^53, whereas [RFC8785] requires
       numbers to be expressible as IEEE 754 doubles and recommends



Kamimura                   Expires 28 May 2027                 [Page 26]

Internet-Draft                  SCITT-VCP                  November 2026


       encoding larger integers as strings.  Values that are not exactly
       representable as a double, such as the TimestampInt
       1773567000123456789 in Appendix A, cannot be canonicalized by RFC
       8785 without changing them.

   4.  EventHash notation.  Examples and fixtures write EventHash with a
       "sha256:" prefix, while the reference computation yields a bare
       hexadecimal digest.

   5.  Security object fields.  This document names the anchor reference
       AnchorRef, lists MerkleRoot as OPTIONAL in individual events, and
       has no MerkleIndex.  In [VCP-SPEC], the specification text names
       the field AnchorReference and lists AnchorReference, MerkleRoot
       and MerkleIndex as REQUIRED from VCP v1.1, whereas the v1.2 JSON
       schema accepts both AnchorRef and AnchorReference and treats the
       three fields as VC-Certified requirements enforced by
       Registration Policy rather than as protocol-level requirements.
       The canonical name and the requirement level are not decided by
       this revision, and the fields here are unchanged.

   6.  Receipt verification context.  Receipt resolution in Section 7.2
       is separate from verification; Section 8.5 builds on inclusion.
       For the RFC9162_SHA256 verifiable data structure, a Receipt of
       inclusion signs the Merkle tree root as its payload, under a
       protected header that identifies the signature algorithm and the
       verifiable data structure; the tree size and leaf index are
       carried in the unprotected header, and verification applies the
       inclusion proof to the bytes of a candidate entry (Section 5.2.1
       of [RFC9942]).  [RFC9943] leaves the specific verifiable data
       structure to the Transparency Service (Section 5.1.3), and
       [RFC9942] notes that leaf construction can differ by data
       structure (Section 4).  This profile does not yet state (a) which
       bytes form the entry for a registered VCP Signed Statement and
       how a verifier learns that rule for a given Transparency Service,
       or (b) which Transparency Service a verifier is expected to trust
       and how it obtains the Receipt verification keys that service
       used, including those for earlier Receipts.  Section 4 of
       [RFC9943] allows the key to change between Receipts, and key
       discovery (Section 2.1 of [I-D.ietf-scitt-scrapi]) does not by
       itself decide which service is trusted.  For (a), the entry rule
       of the CCF profile [CCF-PROFILE] (Sections 2.2 and 3.2: the
       Signed Statement as registered, with tag 18 retained and an empty
       unprotected header, its digest computed by the verifier) is a
       candidate under discussion [SCITT-LIST-OI6].  Under
       RFC9162_SHA256 the vds value in the protected header does not
       identify an entry rule, so a rule stated by this profile would be
       fixed together with the service identity in (b) rather than
       selected by the Receipt.  Revision -06 also left open (c), what a



Kamimura                   Expires 28 May 2027                 [Page 27]

Internet-Draft                  SCITT-VCP                  November 2026


       verifier checks when an argument depends on a statement's
       position in the log or on the log's size.  This revision settles
       (c) by stating in Section 8.5 that the profile relies on Receipts
       for inclusion only.  A future claim resting on position or size
       would need further evidence under the trusted service key, such
       as a signed checkpoint whose root equals the one recomputed from
       the Receipt, together with every entry in the range and
       consistency between the tree heads compared.  This profile also
       does not yet state what a verifier reports when the context in
       (a) or (b) is missing, unknown or contradictory.  Parts (a) and
       (b) are tracked in an intake record [VCP-ISSUE-37]; it adopts no
       change.  The hackathon report presented to the SCITT Working
       Group at IETF 127 names the same points as rules that verifying
       Receipts across operators currently needs out of band
       [SCITT-IETF127].

   Since -05, VSO's change-proposal register has listed proposed
   identifiers for change proposals on items 1 to 5 [VCP-CP-REGISTER]:
   VCP-CP-2026-003 proposes errata notices for items 1 to 3; VCP-CP-
   2026-004 proposes a byte contract and migration that covers items 1
   to 4, with the numeric-field inventory VCP-CP-2026-005 as an input;
   item 5 depends on VCP-CP-2026-002 (precedence among the normative
   annex, the specification text, the JSON schema and examples) and VCP-
   CP-2026-006 (native field layout).  The interim change-control
   procedure under which these proposals would be decided, VCP-CP-
   2026-001, was itself under public review when this revision was
   prepared, and the identifiers take effect only if that procedure is
   accepted.  All of these proposals are unadopted.  This document will
   follow their outcome and does not anticipate it.

   Note to the RFC Editor: please remove this section before

   This section records the status of known implementations at the time

      VCP.

      COSE_Sign1 Signed Statements or register them with a Transparency
      Service, and it does not implement VCP v1.2.  The proof-of-concept

   *  VSO also publishes a first-party, pre-release offline library for
      VCP v1.2 events in Python, TypeScript and MQL5 [VCP-SDK].  It
      computes canonical hashes, RFC 6962 Merkle roots and inclusion and
      consistency proofs, Ed25519 event and root signatures, and reports
      verification results as valid, invalid or indeterminate.  It does
      not read COSE or SCITT envelopes, so it does not implement this
      profile, and it has not been assessed against it.  Where VCP v1.2
      is ambiguous, including item 1 of Section 9, the library records
      its own reading; those readings are not VSO change-control
      decisions.

   *  No Evidence Pack produced under VCP has been accepted in any
      regulatory, judicial, or audit proceeding.
